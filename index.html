01 · The brief

Five questions from
the leadership team.

The leadership team wanted one read of five years of sales: are we growing, where is profit made, and where should effort go next? Each executive brought a sharper version of that question, and each one shaped what I measured.

Who asked what

CEO & boardIs the business growing, and where is the revenue concentrated?
CFOCan we trust the margins we manage the business by?
Sales directorWhich customers and channels are worth the most, and how do we grow them?
Product directorHow exposed is the range to the shift from Traditional to LED lighting?
OperationsWhere does money leak between the order and the invoice?

What changed when I checked the numbers

  • Missing monthsEvery year, 98% of recorded sales fall in January to June, and 7 of the 10 company codes have no July–December lines at all. The "99% collapse" a monthly chart suggests is a gap in the extract, not a fall in sales.
  • Placeholder costsOn 52% of lines the cost equals the quantity, a $1.00 unit cost. The same products carry real costs on other lines, so these are placeholders, and they lift the raw margin to 66%.
  • Intercompany22% of the file is intercompany and head-office sales at a 12.8% margin. Mixed in with customers, it hides how the external business performs.

02 · How I built it

From 1.09M raw lines
to numbers a CFO can sign.

A reusable Python pipeline turns two raw ERP exports into one clean table, and an audit step tests the yardstick before any analysis, because the board acts on the numbers, not on the code.

  • Codes trimmed and standardised; "AUS" mapped to AUD

02

  • Grouped and statistical fallbacks last
  • Zero missing values left
03

Validate

  • Codes tested against 8 code lists in the metadata file
  • 43 lines with invalid codes removed
  • 1,087,121 lines kept
04

Convert

One currency for every line.

  • NZD and USD to AUD at RBA monthly rates
  • Returns and credits kept, not trimmed
  • Skew handled with an arcsinh transform

Test the yardstick.

Blind spot 1: half of every year is missing

Blind spots 2 and 3: costs and intercompany

On 52% of lines the recorded cost equals the quantity sold, which means a unit cost of exactly $1.00. Nine in ten regularly sold products carry real costs on their other lines (the most-ordered product costs $3.06 a unit and sells for $5.65), so these are placeholders, not cheap stock. Left in, they lift the margin to 65.9%.

Taking them out gives 36.1%, but that figure still blends in intercompany and head-office sales, 22% of the file at a 12.8% margin. On external customers alone, gross margin is 42.6%, and that is the number every finding below uses.

03 · What the data says

Six findings for
the leadership team.

Margin basis

External customers only, on lines with a recorded cost. Intercompany and head-office sales are excluded throughout.

Money

AUD. "A year" means the five-year average, 2020–2024, as recorded; second halves are under-reported for most company codes.

Read with care

Patterns are associations in historical sales, not proof of cause. Each one is a hypothesis the 90-day plan tests.
01

Sales are steady, but the mix isn't moving.

Recorded sales held between $244M and $257M a year, with about 3,450 active external customers each year. The mix barely changed: Traditional lighting is 70% of external sales, accessories 18%, and LED 11–12%. LED sales fell from $23.8M in 2020 to $21.7M in 2024.

That matters because regulation is moving faster than the mix. In 2023, parties to the Minamata Convention agreed to end the manufacture, import and export of fluorescent lamps for general lighting by 2027 (CLASP, 2023). Every fluorescent line in the Traditional range now has an end date.

So whatRevenue is stable today, but 70% of it sits in Traditional lighting while LED stands still. The move to LED has to be planned and owned, because the current trend isn't delivering it.
02

Margin is made in the mix and the channel, not in projects.

Accessories earn the best margin of the three product families (44.9%) and LED the lowest (40.0%), so every dollar that moves from Traditional to LED today costs margin. Orders placed by EDI, the electronic reorder channel, earn 45.1%, the best of any channel, yet carry only 4.2% of sales.

Project orders, once read as a 13.7-point premium, earn 43.8% against 44.1% for normal orders once placeholder costs and intercompany sales are removed.

So whatSell LED with accessories and controls rather than as a bare box, move routine reorders onto EDI, and judge projects on their cost to serve, not an assumed premium.
03

A fifth of customers bring 73% of sales.

Ranked by how often they order, the most frequent fifth of the 3,889 external customers place a median of 58 orders a year and spend $116K. The least frequent fifth order less than once a year and spend $369. Across customers, spend rises about one-for-one with order frequency, and the top 10% of customers bring 64% of sales.

Each order from the two most frequent fifths averages about $2,640. At that value, 10% more orders from those 1,556 accounts would add roughly $18M a year in sales, an illustration rather than a forecast.

So whatGrowth is in order frequency from customers already on the books. Protect the top fifth, and move the next fifth up with reorder prompts and EDI.
04

Volume is sold at commodity prices.

Ranking products by units sold, the highest-volume tenth carries 57% of external sales at a median $6.34 a unit. The lowest-volume tenth sells at a median $280. Across products, each doubling of volume comes with a unit price about 25% lower.

So whatThe core of the business competes on price, so small leaks in discounts, freight and small orders move the P&L. Specialised, low-volume lines are where pricing power sits; they need protecting, not discounting.
05

$8M a year flows back as credits.

Credit notes reverse 4.0% of gross external sales, about $8.0M a year. Half is goods coming back ($4.0M). The part to act on first is $2.75M a year of price-only credits: refunds where no goods return, so every dollar comes straight off margin.

Credit rates vary more than margins do: Melbourne credits 4.7% of its sales, against 3.6–4.2% in Sydney, Brisbane and Perth, and the Northern Region of New Zealand 8.8%.

So whatPut price-only credits behind an approval threshold and a monthly review, and find out why Melbourne's and Northern NZ's goods come back.
06

Regions are closer than they look.

On costed lines, the four biggest districts sit within two points of each other: Sydney 43.0%, Perth 42.3%, Brisbane 42.2%, Melbourne 41.3%. Brisbane, once flagged for an urgent margin audit, sits in the middle of the pack. The four smaller Australian districts earn more, 45.2% against 42.2% for the five largest, on far smaller sales.

So whatGeography isn't where margin is won or lost. Spend management attention on channel, credits and product mix, and copy what the regional districts do on price.

04 · The next 90 days

Fix the numbers,
then work the levers.

Each action has an owner, a reason from the data and one measure to track. Finance goes first, because every other decision depends on a margin the business can trust.

The business is managing by a margin that doesn't exist. Three fixes give the board a number it can sign.

01

Restate margin on real costs

Do

Load standard costs for the lines carrying a $1.00 placeholder, and report margin only on costed lines until that's done.

Why

The raw file shows 65.9%; costed lines to external customers show 42.6%. Pricing and bonus decisions made on the higher number are made on a number that doesn't exist.

TrackShare of sales lines with a valid cost
02

Recover the missing half-years

Do

Re-extract July–December for the seven company codes with no second-half lines, then restate annual sales and seasonality.

Why

98% of recorded sales fall in January–June. If that's real, cash and staffing plans must follow it; if it isn't, every annual figure is understated.

TrackCompany codes reporting all 12 months
03

Report customers apart from intercompany

Do

Split intercompany and head-office sales out of every customer KPI and margin report.

Why

They are 22% of the file at a 12.8% margin; blended in, they drag the reported margin and hide how channels perform.

TrackExternal gross margin, monthly

Growth sits with customers already on the books. Protect the most frequent, lift the next tier, and close the credit leak.

04

Protect the top fifth

Do

Name an owner for each of the 778 most frequent accounts, hold quarterly reviews, and flag any account whose gap between orders doubles.

Why

They bring 73% of external sales, and a lapse is invisible until month-end totals fall.

TrackOrders per top-fifth account, per quarter
05

Move the next fifth up

Do

Reorder reminders and EDI onboarding for the next 778 accounts, which order a median of 17 times a year.

Why

EDI orders earn 45.1%, the best of any channel, but carry 4.2% of sales. 10% more orders from the top two fifths would add about $18M a year at today's order value.

TrackEDI share of external sales
06

Put price-only credits behind approval

Do

Require sign-off above a set value, and review price-only credits monthly by salesperson and customer.

Why

$2.75M a year is credited with no goods returned, so it comes straight off margin.

TrackPrice-only credits per month

Two kinds of product need two kinds of pricing. Hold the line on the commodity core, and stop giving away the specialised tail.

07

Set floors for the commodity core

Do

Floor prices, minimum order quantities and freight thresholds for the highest-volume tenth of products.

Why

It carries 57% of external sales at a median $6.34 a unit, so every cent of discount is multiplied by volume.

TrackMargin on the top-volume tenth
08

Value-price the specialised tail

Do

Remove standard discounts from low-volume specialised lines and quote them on fit and application.

Why

The lowest-volume tenth sells at a median $280 a unit, against $6.34 for the highest-volume tenth; buyers of specialised lighting pay for the right product, not the cheapest.

TrackAverage discount on low-volume lines
09

Sell LED as a solution, not a box

Do

Bundle LED with accessories and controls, and quote retrofits as projects with a cost-to-serve check.

Why

LED alone earns 40.0%, the lowest family; accessories earn 44.9%, the highest.

TrackLED margin and accessory attach rate

The three blind spots were invisible in standard reports. Make the checks routine, then chase the returns.

10

Run the pipeline every month

Do

Schedule the cleaning pipeline monthly, with alerts for placeholder costs, missing months, invalid codes and unconverted currencies.

Why

One unchecked extract produced a 99% fall that never happened. The same checks catch the next one before it reaches the board.

TrackData checks passed each month
11

Find out why goods come back

Do

Add a return reason to every goods-return credit, starting with Melbourne and Northern NZ.

Why

Returns cost $4.0M a year. Melbourne credits 4.7% of its sales against 3.6–4.2% in the other big metros, and Northern NZ 8.8%.

TrackCredit rate by district
12

Plan the peak once the data is confirmed

Do

If the January–June concentration proves real, set stock, staffing and cash plans around it.

Why

A business that earns 98% of its sales in six months runs on very different working capital from one that sells evenly.

TrackFill rate in peak months

What I'd confirm before the board decides

  • Whether July–December is missing from the extract or genuinely quiet; finance can answer this in a day.
  • The standard cost for every product with a $1.00 placeholder, before any price change.
  • How intercompany prices are set, since 22% of the file moves at a 12.8% margin.
  • That patterns are associations, not causes: each lever is tested with a pilot and a comparison group.

How I take a messy extract to a board decision

Audit the yardstick first

The "99% collapse" was a missing half-year. Checking what a number is made of comes before explaining why it moved.

A margin is only as good as its cost

Half the cost lines were placeholders. One check moved the margin from 66% to 43% and reversed the project finding.

Separate customers from the company

Intercompany sales are a fifth of the file. Customer decisions need customer numbers.

Every recommendation gets an owner

Each action names who acts, why the data says so, and one measure that shows whether it worked.
